# Validate idea: Build Teams

Assumption: zero real users today (stated in VISION.md, 2026-10-02).
Assumption: solo founder, part time, project is also a portfolio piece.
Assumption: the buyer is the developer, not an employer.

## 1. Gut reaction
The problem is real: juniors lack shared-codebase experience. But this is a feature of Vahoha, not a business, and it cannot start until Vahoha has capstone passers, of whom there are none. The riskiest part is people, not code.

## 2. Five fatal questions

**Q1: Customer and payment.** A self-taught backend developer, 0-3 years in, trying to move to mid-level. Willingness to pay for teams specifically: unknown. Boot.dev charges $59/month ([source](https://www.boot.dev/blog/education/code-academy-alternatives)), so paying for structured backend learning exists, but nobody has shown they pay for matched teams. "Maybe" is a no.

**Q2: Why not built already.** Many have tried team formation for learners. Buildspace ran cohort projects for about 125,000 builders and shut down in August 2024, for personal reasons given by the founder, not failed demand ([source](https://newsletter.failory.com/p/hidden-struggles)). Free options exist: HackLab lets developers pitch and join projects ([source](https://github.com/acm-projects/HackLab)), and student clubs run team projects ([source](https://www.binghamton.edu/news/story/5438/stackhacks-fosters-computer-science-skills-teamwork-and-career-connections)). What is different here: a verified baseline (capstone pass), per-member reviewed and defended evidence. That is the only real edge.

**Q3: Distribution.** First 100 users: none have a path to teams until they pass a capstone. Growth loop: finished projects and demos could bring the next cohort, but that loop is unproven. Today every user would come from manual outreach.

**Q4: Big business or feature?** A feature. Ceiling as a standalone: small. For $1M ARR you would need about 5,600 paying users at $15 a month, and that needs the core Vahoha loop to work first. The larger business is the evidence profile that employers use. Teams feed it.

**Q5: Can the founder build it?** Yes on code: capstone, defense, GitHub client and mastery data already exist. Hardest part is operational: keeping strangers committed for weeks. Not a first-hire problem.

## 4. Experiments

1. **Interest test.** Post the offer ("4-person backend team, 6-week project, reviewed and defended") in two communities. Success: 12 sign-ups in 10 days. Cost: $0.
2. **Hand-run team.** 4 volunteers, one brief, chat group, shared repo, you review. Success: 3 of 4 active in week 4 and all 4 pass a defense. Time: 6 weeks of calendar time, 2 hours a week from you. Cost: $0.
3. **Employer signal.** Show 5 hiring managers or senior developers one member's evidence page (tasks, reviews, defense). Success: 3 say it would change whether they interview the person. Cost: $0.

## 5. Verdict: pivot
Do not build Build Teams as a product now. Make the defended, reviewed evidence per person the core, and run teams as one way to produce it. A cheaper first version is a review exchange: two learners review and defend each other's capstones. Run experiment 3 first, because if employers do not care, none of the rest matters.
